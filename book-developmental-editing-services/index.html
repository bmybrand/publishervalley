The View From Outside

The Gap Between Intention and Execution

There is a version of your story that only you know. Like the chapter that feels slow, but eventually pays off fifty pages later. Your reader knows none of that. And neither does your developmental editor. That is not a limitation. It is the point.

What The Author Sees

You have lived with the story for months. Maybe years. You know what every scene is supposed to mean. You can fill in the missing pieces without even noticing they are missing because your mind supplies them automatically. The reader sees a character disappear. And this is where being close to your own manuscript becomes dangerous.

What The Editor Sees

A developmental editor starts with none of that knowledge. They do not know your private version of the story. They have only the manuscript. So when they say, “The middle loses momentum here,” they are not necessarily saying it is badly written. They may be showing you that the reason exists in your head but never made it onto the page.

What Happens When The Story’s Problems Go Untouched

A manuscript can have clean sentences and still be broken underneath. Line editing and copy editing can only clean up the mechanics. Neither can save a plot that falls apart. Here’s the difference developmental editing makes.

Without Developmental Editing

  • ✗Plot holes and weak story logic stay buried until a reader or agent finds them.
  • ✗Slow chapters get cleaner sentences while the real problem sits underneath them.
  • ✗Characters change, and important arcs never reach the place they were built to reach.
  • ✗Themes and subplots are introduced with promise, then quietly abandoned.
  • ✗Structural changes force you to rewrite scenes that were already line edited.

With Developmental Editing

  • ✓Major problems are identified before you spend time polishing sentences.
  • ✓Chapters and acts are examined as part of the larger story.
  • ✓Their choices build toward their arcs instead of changing.
  • ✓Themes and story threads are tracked through the manuscript.
  • ✓Once the structure is working, line editing and copy editing can focus on strengthening.

Our Sample Edit Policy

A Developmental Edit Cannot Be Judged From Ten Pages

Choosing a developmental edit from a sample is like judging an entire restaurant by its appetizers. The first few bites might be excellent. The kitchen could still lose the plot by the main course.

Because developmental editing depends on things a ten-page excerpt simply cannot show:

  • •Does the story actually hold from the first page to the last?
  • •Do the things introduced early actually matter?
  • •Do character changes make sense across the manuscript?
  • •Where does the story slow down or lose its grip?
  • •Do the deeper ideas build naturally or disappear somewhere?
  • •Do timelines hold together from chapter to chapter?
  • •Do the acts carry the story forward or start pulling it apart?

Giving you a ten-page developmental sample would mean judging a book before we have actually read it. We would rather not pretend.

What we offer instead:

FAQs

Developmental Editing Questions, Answered

What Is Developmental Editing and What Does a Developmental Editor Do?
Developmental editing inspects what holds the book together, like plot and structure, etc. It finds the bigger problems in your plot formation and how you have placed scenes.
When Should I Get A Developmental Edit?
After you have a complete draft and before line editing or copy editing. Because, to be fair, there is no point working with the sentences and lines you may end up cutting.
Does My Manuscript Need Developmental Editing?
If something feels wrong but you cannot put your finger on it, and you are doubting your story plotting. Then this is your cue to get a developmental edit.
How Much Does Developmental Editing Cost?
Your manuscript actually decides the price. We look at its word count and the work, like how many rounds of reading it may require, before giving you a custom quote.
How Long Would My Developmental Edit Take?
Most developmental edits take several weeks, but there is no fixed timeline. We never promise one timeline. Once you hire us, we will give you a time frame for each round to expect.
Do Your Developmental Editing Services Include An Editorial Letter?
Yes. You will receive a detailed editorial letter, which is more like a report of 20-25 pages or more depending on your story. Covering the major issues in your manuscript.
Can I Get Developmental Editing For Nonfiction?
Absolutely. We work on memoir, academic, Christian, business, and other nonfiction manuscripts as well.
Copy Editing vs. Developmental Editing: How Are They Different?
Developmental editing fixes problems with the book itself, like this specific scene should have been in the starting chapters. Whereas copy editing means fixing typos and grammar.
Should I Get Developmental Editing Before Line Editing?
If the story still needs structural work, yes. Fix the story first, then worry about making every sentence sharper. We can help you with both, developmental editors and line editors as well.
What If I Don't Agree With My Editor's Suggestions?
You do not have to accept everything. Your editor explains the reasoning, and you decide what belongs in your book.
