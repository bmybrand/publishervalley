The Purpose of Line Editing

One of the most common questions we hear in book editing is, “What is line editing?” Does it mean changing the whole line? The answer is simple. Line editing is where we work on the writing itself. Not the plot. Not the big story decisions. The sentences.

And that does not mean changing what you wanted to say in a line or a paragraph. It simply means saying it in a more sentimental way. For example, using more compelling words in a sentence, changing the rhythm, or eliminating lines that make the sentence feel dragged.

We do not rewrite the book in our voice. We strengthen yours. And we do it before copyediting and proofreading services. Because there is no point fixing the grammar of a sentence you are about to rewrite anyway.

What We Check During Line Editing

  • Voice & ToneWe catch places where the writing suddenly sounds too formal or unlike you.
  • Sentence FlowAdjusting long and choppy sentences so the writing has a more natural rhythm.
  • ClarityIf a sentence makes the reader reread it, we find what is getting in the way.
  • Text EmphasisWhere capitals or italics help the reader, such as whispered words or a character’s thoughts.
  • DialogueCharacters should not sound like they are giving speeches when having a conversation.
  • Filler and RepetitionIf a sentence works without “really,” “just,” or “very,” those words go.
  • TransitionsWe fix the gaps where the writing jumps from one thought to another.
  • Showing, Not TellingInstead of saying “he was sad,” we look for ways to let the scene show it.

Line Editing vs In-Line Editing

The names sound almost the same. The work is not. Line editing is what we do to the writing. We work through the sentences to fix weak wording and tone. Inline editing is where you see those changes. The inline editor makes them directly in your manuscript. Our line edits come back in a Word document with the changes shown where they happen.

The Rules We Keep Track Of

A few points our line editors keep in mind while working on projects.

Point Of View
First person, present tense, told through their perspective.
Spelling
“Colour,” not “color,” based on the author’s preference.
Capitalization
Capitalize “The Council” when referring to the ruling group.
Names & Places
“Blackwater,” “North Gate,” and “The Old Mill” stay written the same.
Character Voice
Marcus speaks formally. Lily uses shorter and simpler sentences.
Intentional Style
Short fragments are kept in tense scenes to control the pace.

Every line edit comes with a style sheet like this. Your choices stay locked in through copyediting and proofreading.

Different Ways to Get Your Book Edited

Every editing stage has a different job. Start with the wrong one, and you might end up spending money polishing problems that still need to be fixed.

    Developmental Editing

    Working on the problems underneath the writing, like plot, pacing, and characters.

  2. Stage 2

    Substantive Editing

    Eliminating what is not working and moving words that are out of place.

You may see “developmental editing” and “substantive editing” used as if they mean the same thing. We treat them as two different stages. Developmental editing finds what is wrong. Substantive editing fixes it inside the manuscript. The difference between line editing and copyediting is that one makes it sound more interesting, and the other catches the errors that remain.

Line Editing vs Copy Editing On The Same Passage

Original

“I don’t think we should be here,” Beatrice said quietly as she looked down the dark hallway. “It’s getting really late and everyone else have gone home. She looked back at Marco who was standing near the door. “Did you hear that sound just now?

Copy Editing asks: Is it correct?

“I don’t think we should be here,” Beatrice said quietly as she looked down the dark hallway. “It’s getting really late, and everyone else havehas gone home.” She looked back at Marco, who was standing near the door. “Did you hear that sound just now?”

The grammar and punctuation are now correct. The sentence still reads the same, but the errors are gone.

Line Editing asks: Does it read well?

“I don’t think we should be here,” Beatrice said quietly as she looked down the dark hallway. The hallway stretched into darkness. “It’s getting really late and everyone else have gone home.Everyone else had already gone home.” She looked back at Marco who was standing near the door.Then something creaked upstairs. Beatrice looked at Marco. “Did you hear that sound just now?You heard that, right?”

The longer dialogue is broken into shorter lines, and the extra explanation is cut so the tension builds naturally.

Every Genre Needs a Different Kind of Editorial Eye

Line editing is not the same for every genre. Your editor should understand what your genre demands. More importantly, they should know what to leave alone.

  • Fiction Line Editing

    A good story can still lose its grip when the prose gets in the way. We tighten slow scenes and fix sentences that pull attention away from the story.

    • Dialogue
    • Scene pacing
    • Point of view

  • Literary Fiction Line Editing

    Literary writing has its own rhythm. We do not flatten that voice just to make it sound “cleaner.” We refine the lines that feel unclear and protect the choices that make the writing yours.

    • Imagery
    • Sentence rhythm
    • Authorial voice

  • Fantasy Book Line Editing

    A fantasy world can fall apart when the writing makes readers work too hard to understand it. We clean up heavy exposition and keep details consistent throughout the manuscript.

    • World-building
    • Exposition
    • Action clarity

  • Non-Fiction Line Editing

    Good information means nothing in non-fiction if readers have to fight through it. Our experts understand it and cut the clutter to make each section easier to follow.

    • Clarity
    • Structure
    • Jargon

  • Memoir Line Editing

    Your memoir should not sound like it was written by an editor. It should sound like you. We just strengthen your emotional moments while keeping your voice and personality untouched.

    • Personal voice
    • Tense consistency
    • Emotional pacing

Line Editing Questions, Answered

The questions worth asking before you hand your manuscript over for a closer look.

What Is Line Editing And What Does It Do To A Manuscript?+

Line editing gets into the writing itself. We find the sentences that drag too much and take away the story's worth. Then we work to fix prose that loses its grip.

How Is Line Editing Different From Copy Editing?+

Line editing deals with how your writing reads. However, copy editing is about catching grammar and consistency problems.

When Should I Get My Book Line Edited?+

Get a line edit done once you are done with developmental editing. Shape up the plot and structure. Then work to get the lines more well-written and impactful.

Do I Need Both Line Editing And Copy Editing?+

In most cases, yes. One strengthens the writing. The other cleans up what is left behind.

Will I Be Able To See What The Editor Changes?+

Yes. Every change is visible in Word through Track Changes, so nothing gets changed behind your back.

Can I Question Or Reject An Editor’s Changes?+

Absolutely. You wrote the book. You get the final say on what stays and what goes.

Will Line Editing Change My Voice?+

It shouldn’t. We are here to fix the writing that gets in the way, not scrub your voice out of the book.

How Much Do Your Line Editing Services Cost?+

It all depends on the length of your manuscript and how much work it needs. Send us your word count, and we’ll give you a custom quote.

How Long Does Line Editing For Books Take?+

That depends on the manuscript. A clean 60,000-word book and a messy 60,000-word book are two very different jobs.

Do You Offer Fiction And Non-Fiction Line Editing?+

Yes. We edit both fiction and non-fiction across different genres. Your editor is matched to the kind of book you wrote.

Is My Manuscript Kept Confidential?+

Yes. Your manuscript stays private while it is with us. Your words and your rights stay yours.
